## Summary

This week's most useful result is a diagnosis of how sparse attention fails.
In block-pooled selectors such as InfLLM-V2 and NSA, multi-key recall is lost
in a handful of heads, and a cheap targeted fix restores almost all of it.
Theory came from two directions:
- A group-theory result says diagonal (real or complex) state space models can
  only track commutative structure.
- A Hankel-norm argument says retrieving one specific token from a linear
  time-invariant filter cannot be compressed.

No paper moved the recall-versus-state frontier at matched state size. The
closest, bilateral log-linear attention, gains a lot of recall while roughly
doubling its state.

## Worth your attention

### 1. Lost in a Few Heads: block selection in sparse attention

Paper: Zhang & Chen, arXiv:2611.21917, 16 Nov 2026.

- **Card:** [attention efficiency](../cards/attention-efficiency.md), open
  question "How does sparse attention fail?"
- **Classification:** answers an open question, plus a new boundary.

Selectors that score blocks from mean-pooled keys rank the block holding the
needle too low when look-alike blocks compete with it. The authors prove a
"blind window" in a stylised two-block logit model, where pooling must
misrank, `[proven, if logits follow the two-block model]`.

In the InfLLM-V2 / MiniCPM4 family, the loss sits in three heads (L9.h0,
L16.h0, L22.h0). On RULER MK3 at 64K, giving those heads full attention, or a
4-bit scorer at the shipped budget, lifts MiniCPM4.1-8B from 54.1 to 97.5,
against 97.1 dense. Qwen3-8B with the SeerAttention-R gate goes from 29.8 to
97.7 when 18 of its 288 KV heads get full attention. `[empirical]`, measured
on one model lineage for the three-head result.

Token-level selectors (DeepSeek-V3.2 DSA, LongCat-Flash-Lite-Sparse) showed no
multi-key loss. That was measured on one near-ceiling task each.

Code: <https://github.com/zhangwenhe1007/blind-attention-heads>

### 2. Symmetry in Time: representation theory of linear recurrent memory

Paper: Shikder, Dey, Mináč & Muller, arXiv:2611.17162, 12 Nov 2026.

- **Card:** [state space models](../cards/state-space-models.md)
- **Classification:** new boundary, plus a new lineage link (group
  representation theory).

The state update of a linear recurrence is a representation of the input
monoid. A channel can track a group only if that group is a quotient of the
group its transitions generate. It follows that diagonal real or complex SSMs
(the Mamba-1/2 style) track only abelian groups. A stack of commutative layers
tracks a finite group only if the group is solvable, with derived length at
most the depth, so it never reaches S5. `[proven, within the model: compact
reachable states, continuous readout, no decay]`

One quaternion channel (4 numbers of state) tracks A5. On S5 with 32
generators, it is exact up to 25 times the training length. PD-SSM and Gated
DeltaProduct baselines score 1.07% and 1.23% on that task, against 0.83%
chance (baseline numbers copied from prior work). `[empirical]`, synthetic
tasks only.

The paper names whether this helps large models as its main open question.

Code: <https://github.com/anifshikder/HyperSSM>

### 3. Bilateral Log-Linear Attention

Paper: Islam & Cong, arXiv:2611.18974, 13 Nov 2026.

- **Card:** [attention efficiency](../cards/attention-efficiency.md), open
  question "Does any sub-linear-memory architecture beat the Based frontier?"
- **Classification:** claims to move a frontier, but not at matched state.

Forward log-linear attention summarises early context coarsely. This paper adds
a second, origin-anchored hierarchy of state matrices, which keeps `O(log T)`
state at about twice the constant.

| Benchmark | Mamba-2 | Log-linear | Bilateral | Transformer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MQAR, 512 tokens (overall) | 70.5 | 76.4 | 96.1 | 99.5 |
| RULER-NIAH average at 6144 (about 365M parameters) | 18.4 | 27.0 | 50.0 | 58.7 |

`[empirical]`, with these limits:
- One training seed for the language models.
- Small models, no runs longer than 8K tokens, no throughput numbers.
- No Gated DeltaNet baseline.

Because the extra recall arrives with extra state, this is evidence for
"state that grows slowly helps". It does not yet show a new frontier.

## Also relevant

- **SinkState** (Jiang et al., arXiv:2611.14246).
  - Finding: in production hybrids (Nemotron-3, Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3-Next), the
    queries, keys and values of linear-attention layers lie in a stable
    low-dimensional subspace. Keeping each recurrent state in low-rank form
    cuts peak state memory 1.9x on average, with at most −0.1 on RULER NIAH
    at 16K. `[empirical]`
  - Bears on [test-time training](../cards/test-time-training.md) "recall per
    byte of state", and is an adoption signal.
- **Lost in Rounding** (Weng, Chen & Liu, arXiv:2611.12127).
  - Finding: storing Mamba-2 or Gated DeltaNet state in bf16 with
    round-to-nearest makes 5 of 6 hybrid reasoning models think 7–27% longer.
    Qwen3.5-9B math accuracy falls from 85.6% to 70.8%. Stochastic rounding at
    the same width mostly fixes it. `[empirical]`, single seed for most arms.
  - A measurement trap for any hybrid comparison.
- **Kernel, Transport, Support** (Pei, Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.24191078,
  not on arXiv, single author, no experiments).
  - Finding: attention, SSMs and delta-rule mixers fit one formula. A
    Hankel-singular-value argument shows that retrieving one specific token
    through an `n`-state time-invariant filter is incompressible, while
    averaging is cheap. `[proven, for linear time-invariant filters only]`;
    it does not cover data-dependent Gated DeltaNet or Mamba-2.
  - This is a control-theory link for the SSM card.
- **TANSAR** (Abdulla et al., arXiv:2611.12374).
  - Finding: deterministic crosstalk and capacity bounds for additive
    (Hebbian) correlation matrix memories with correlated keys. No key set
    keeps mean squared crosstalk at or below `ε²` beyond `n ≤ d(1+ε²)`
    associations. `[proven, for unit keys and values]`
  - Semantic keys cut capacity at `ε = 0.5` from 257 to 58 against random
    keys. `[empirical]`, `d = 1024`.
  - No delta-rule comparison, so it only half answers the
    [associative memory](../cards/associative-memory.md) question.
- **The Kernel Reality Check** (Öncel et al., arXiv:2611.22947, NeurIPS 2026).
  - Finding: on one A100, FlashAttention beats Hedgehog and DiJiang on time
    and memory everywhere except causal-LM inference memory. Distilled kernel
    methods trail softmax by 2–10 points. `[empirical]`, models up to 774M
    parameters.
  - No recall benchmarks were run.
- **Information Limits of Replay-Free Recurrent State Migration** (Qureshi,
  arXiv:2611.19345).
  - Finding: exact minimum memory needed to swap a linear recurrent model's
    parameters without replaying history. `[proven, for linear time-invariant
    models]`
  - Niche, but another state-capacity bound.
- **Weight-Space Programming Doesn't Need Transformers** (Ok & Lee,
  arXiv:2611.22055).
  - Finding: when frontier LLMs write weights by hand, they all choose a sparse
    associative table over a Transformer. On ARC multiple choice, a 6.24M
    table model scores 52.4%, against 41.4% for a same-size Transformer, and
    runs 17x faster. Both are far below pretrained LLMs. `[empirical]`
  - A lineage note on hard versus soft lookup.
- **Microfitting** (Ryan et al., arXiv:2611.20258).
  - Finding: "test-time training" here means a cached LoRA adapter per
    document on DeepSeek V4 Flash, not a test-time-training layer.
  - A naming trap for the [test-time training](../cards/test-time-training.md)
    card.

## Summary

The main news for your cards concerns the write rule of fixed-state memory.
Two papers replace the delta rule with a richer update: a full
recursive-least-squares covariance in Kernel Ridge Attention, and a two-layer
MLP state in Toaster. Both win at matched parameters but not at matched state,
and Toaster's own state-matched check shows that most of its gain comes from
the larger state.

On the multi-agent card, a synthesis of 516 published configurations finds
that, against baselines matched on samples or budget, multi-agent systems
average about 4 points below the baseline. This supports the card's headline
that voting explains most of the gain.

Attention at Absolute Zero shows the softmax temperature dial at work:
retrieval that works at training size breaks as the number of items grows,
unless the read is pushed to the zero-temperature limit. This is the β story
from the associative-memory card, measured in a new setting.

## Worth your attention

### 1. Kernel Ridge Attention: covariance-guided writes for recurrent memory

Paper: Chen, Wang & Xu, arXiv:2611.23896, 18 Nov 2026.

- **Card:** [associative memory](../cards/associative-memory.md), open
  questions "delta-rule versus Hebbian capacity" and "does delta-rule writing
  recover capacity lost to correlated keys?"; also
  [test-time training](../cards/test-time-training.md).
- **Classification:** sharpens an open question, plus a new lineage link
  (Kalman filtering and Gaussian-process regression). It claims a better
  trade-off, but not at matched state.

The delta rule writes the error along the key direction. KRA keeps a covariance
of past keys and writes along `P·k` instead, so directions already pinned down
by earlier, correlated keys are changed less. That is recursive least squares,
the exact solution the delta rule only approximates. A learned decay turns it
into a Kalman filter, and the authors prove that the output equals kernel ridge
regression over the prefix with a content kernel times an exponential time
kernel (Proposition 2) `[proven]`. This is the card's own `[speculation]` that
"least squares compensates for correlated keys", built as a layer.

Results at about 31M parameters and 100M tokens, from one seed
`[empirical, self-reported]`:

| Model | Perplexity | Rare-repeat top-1 | State at 8K |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transformer++ | 97.21 | 32.00% | 72 MiB |
| Gated DeltaNet | 110.95 | 6.69% | 0.615 MiB |
| KRA | 88.00 | 25.41% | 1.165 MiB |

The state is about 1.9 times Gated DeltaNet's, there is no MQAR test, and the
scale is tiny. So this shows the direction is promising, not that the frontier
moved.

### 2. More agents, more samples, or more thinking?

Paper: Sajjadi Mohammadabadi, arXiv:2611.17610, 13 Nov 2026 (single author;
code at github.com/mahmoudsajjadi/pace).

- **Card:** [multi-agent communication](../cards/multi-agent-communication.md),
  open question "does Stream of Belief beat an `N × K`-sample vote at equal
  compute?"
- **Classification:** sharpens an open question (empirical), plus a small
  boundary.

The paper pools nine published studies, 516 multi-agent configurations in
total.
- Against a single greedy pass, multi-agent accuracy differs by −0.032 on
  average, or −0.005 without the largest study.
- Against baselines matched on sample count or budget, the average is −0.042,
  and the multi-agent system equals or trails the baseline in 74% of 474
  contrasts.
- A multilevel model on the log-odds scale gives −0.195, CI [−0.320, −0.070].

`[empirical]`: not a systematic search, high heterogeneity (I² = 93%), and
labelled exploratory by the author.

Proposition 3.1 shows that communication adds no information when all agents
see the same evidence `[proven, if messages are functions of shared evidence
plus independent noise]`. The author notes that this says nothing about
compute, so it does not rule out debate beating voting at equal tokens.

All the included methods talk in text; Stream of Belief and other latent
methods are not covered. The open question therefore stays open, but the bar
it must clear is now clear: a budget-matched sampled baseline.

### 3. Attention at Absolute Zero

Paper: Mohsenvand, arXiv:2611.11937 (v3, 19 Nov 2026; v1 was titled
"Attention is only as good as its witnesses"). Single author; the paper says
its code, proofs and drafts were produced by AI systems under the author's
direction.

- **Cards:** [associative memory](../cards/associative-memory.md) (β and
  metastable blending) and [attention efficiency](../cards/attention-efficiency.md).
- **Classification:** new lineage link (tropical, max-plus algebra), plus a new
  boundary.

The paper names three ways soft attention fails as the number of items grows,
all shown empirically:
- weight disperses over many items;
- learned scores are too close together to separate;
- a soft state drifts over loops.

A temperature tuned for 8 items fails from 48. Scaling the scores up (that is,
raising β) helps step by step: 72.0, 83.1, 98.7 and 99.7%. The zero-temperature
read gets 99.8%.

Proposition 1 `[proven]`: as the temperature goes to zero, a softmax sum keeps
only the best score and how many items tie with it. The proposed layer reads
exactly that.

Results `[empirical, self-reported]`:
- Maximum retrieval: at least 99.95% correct at up to 16,384 items, on ten
  seeds.
- CLRS-30 without hints: 97.04%, against 96.64% for the best published model.
- Beside a frozen Qwen2.5-7B: chains family relations exactly at 1,000
  statements after training on 16, where the model alone scores 43.75%.

Theorem 5 says the layer decides a constraint class at every size if and only
if the class has bounded width, so it provably cannot do parity `[proven,
resting on Barto's and Atserias–Bulatov–Dawar's theorems]`.

Costs: `O(n³)` per loop on a pair state, and some design choices were made
after seeing results (97.98% falls to 94.77% with the first-registered
choices).

For the vault, this is the "large β gives clean lookup, small β gives a blend"
trade-off from Experiment 2 of the Hopfield explorer, shown to matter at scale.

## Also relevant

- **Toaster** (Li, Behrouz, Deng, Kacham, Al-Rfou, Razaviyayn & Mirrokni,
  Google Research, arXiv:2611.23648).
  - Two delta-rule memories in series with a GELU between them, trained by
    target propagation: an MLP-state memory that runs on the KDA kernel.
  - At 1.2B parameters, C4 perplexity is 9.94, against 10.00 for KDA and 9.96
    for attention.
  - The state is 8 times KDA's. At 145M, KDA given the same 8× state reaches
    15.79, against Toaster's 15.69, so most of the gain is state size.
  - No MQAR. On RULER S-NIAH at 4K, Toaster scores 100/62.7/27.3 against KDA's
    100/37.3/28.0. Single seed. `[empirical, self-reported]`
  - Bears on [test-time training](../cards/test-time-training.md) "recall per
    byte of state, MLP versus matrix", and is the matched-state check the
    associative-memory card asks for.
- **Selectivity bar for revising answers** (Tang & Lin, arXiv:2611.16694,
  NeurIPS 2026).
  - For a binary decision with initial accuracy `a`, revision raises accuracy
    only if wrong answers are reversed more than `a/(1−a)` times as often as
    correct ones: nine times at 90%. `[proven]` as an accounting identity for
    binary decisions; not stated as a theorem.
  - Under disagreement attributed to an expert, no model of five clears the
    bar. Claude-Sonnet-4 loses 16.1 points. `[empirical]`, 1,989 items.
  - The same test applies to every debate round on the
    [multi-agent card](../cards/multi-agent-communication.md).
- **Latent feedback** (Huang, Dwivedi, Ma, Moreno & Liu, arXiv:2611.20404).
  - Feeds each token's top-layer state into the next token's input, trained
    with fixed-point passes.
  - At equal training FLOPs (including the extra pass), it saves up to 48% of
    compute or about 47% of unique data. It loses below about 33 unique tokens
    per parameter at two epochs. `[empirical, self-reported]`, 60M–778M, three
    seeds at one size only.
  - Pretraining loss only: no chain-of-thought test and no Coconut comparison.
    So it does not answer the [latent reasoning](../cards/latent-reasoning.md)
    question, but it is the first matched-compute win for hidden-state
    feedback the vault has seen.
- **Rethinking Fixed-Horizon Latent Reasoning** (Chen et al.,
  arXiv:2611.24001, abstract only).
  - Running latent chain of thought to its full preset length can lower
    accuracy: later steps reshuffle candidates and weaken the right one.
  - Stopping adaptively gives up to +7.2 points with 29% less latent compute,
    on arithmetic benchmarks only.
- **Surfacing Disagreement Beats Resolving It** (Khan et al.,
  arXiv:2611.22461, abstract only).
  - In debate with shared memory, passing the set of conflicting facts to the
    agents, instead of picking a winner, gives +0.340 F1 at equal cost. It
    also cuts poisoned-memory attacks from 22 to 7 of 122.
  - Fits the multi-agent card's "debate helps when it surfaces something the
    vote missed".
- **FlashMorph2** (Lan et al., arXiv:2611.24809, abstract only).
  - Chooses which layers keep full attention when converting a pretrained
    Qwen3 model to a hybrid, by evaluating sampled hybrids end to end.
  - +2.42 on RULER over FlashMorph.
  - Relevant to the [attention efficiency](../cards/attention-efficiency.md)
    question about the minimum fraction of exact-access layers.
- **V-JEPA-LM** (Kim, Kim & Choi, arXiv:2611.17831, abstract only).
  - A linear probe reads speed from frozen V-JEPA features at 87%, while the
    video-language model built on it answers at chance.
  - Dynamics sit in a shared subspace for predictive encoders but not for
    SigLIP-style ones.
  - For the [vision encoder](../cards/vision-encoder.md) card: the kind of
    prior matters for what can be read, and the supervision decides what is
    read.
- **Looped models as latent depth** (abstracts only):
  - Loopback Transformer (Ouyang et al., arXiv:2611.07226).
  - Late Entry in Looped Language Models (Jin et al., arXiv:2611.24576).
  - A lineage note for [latent reasoning](../cards/latent-reasoning.md):
    recurrence in depth rather than across tokens.
- **Chorus** (Chung & Du, arXiv:2611.23386, abstract only).
  - One shared n-gram embedding table read differently by each layer matches
    DeepSeek's Engram with 87% smaller tables.
  - A parametric associative memory, which bears on the associative-memory
    card's "three memories in one model".
- **Mixture of experts** (Jha, arXiv:2611.20419, abstract only).
  - Pruning three quarters of the routed experts in a 122B reasoning MoE causes
    verbatim reasoning loops.
  - Rank-8 corrections recover +9.2 points on pre-registered LiveCodeBench
    problems. One model only.
- **Aligning AI Teams** (Srinivasan, Matthews, Sohl-Dickstein & Jones,
  arXiv:2611.17820, abstract only).
  - Safety behaviour of single agents fails to carry over to teams, mostly
    through diffusion of responsibility.
  - A side note for the multi-agent card.
- **Adoption signals** (alphaXiv metadata only; not yet in Semantic Scholar):
  - TideCache, hybrid-model serving (arXiv:2611.26619).
  - FrontierReplay, exact prefix caching for hybrids (arXiv:2611.26020).
  - Twindex (arXiv:2611.25078). Its abstract names Qwen3.8-Flash-Next,
    DeepSeek V4.1-Flash and GLM-5.2 as shipped sparse-attention models with
    token-level indexers.

## New questions

- At matched state bytes, does a covariance-guided (recursive least squares)
  write beat the delta rule on MQAR capacity, especially with correlated keys?
- Toaster's gain mostly disappears at matched state. Does KRA's also
  disappear?
- Does any debate protocol, latent or text, clear the `a/(1−a)` selectivity
  bar in each round?
- Does a zero-temperature or adaptive-β read help associative recall in
  language models, or only on exact algorithmic tasks?
- Does hidden-state feedback that helps pretraining (2611.20404) also give
  Coconut-style reasoning gains without a separate latent training stage?
